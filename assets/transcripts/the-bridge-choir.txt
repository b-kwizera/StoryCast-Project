Every Thursday evening, beneath the old railway bridge, thirty voices begin to warm up.
The choir started eight years ago, when a rainstorm forced a few neighbours to shelter in the same spot.
Someone hummed to pass the time. Someone else joined in. By the end of the storm, they had a harmony.
Now the choir has grandparents, students, bus drivers, and one very serious ten year old tenor.
The bridge gives their sound a long, warm echo that no concert hall could copy.
They never perform for money. They sing so the neighbourhood remembers it can still make something together.
If you walk by on a Thursday, stop and listen. They will always save you a place.
