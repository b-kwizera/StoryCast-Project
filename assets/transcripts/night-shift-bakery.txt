At midnight, when most of the city is asleep, the ovens at Baraka Bakery are already hot.
Joseph kneads the dough by hand, because he says the bread can feel when it is rushed.
His apprentice, Grace, learned to read the dough by touch, long before she learned the recipes.
Together they bake nearly three hundred loaves before dawn.
Many go to the school down the road, and to the clinic where the nurses start their early shift.
Joseph believes a warm loaf is a small promise that someone was thinking of you.
By six o'clock, the shelves are full, the ovens cool, and the night shift finally goes home.
